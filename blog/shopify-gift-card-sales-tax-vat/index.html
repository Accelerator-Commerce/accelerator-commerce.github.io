Do You Need to Charge Tax on a Shopify Gift Card? US vs. EU Rules

Short answer: In the US, you generally don't charge sales tax when a gift card is purchased — tax applies later, when it's redeemed for taxable goods. In the EU, it's more complicated: gift cards that can only be spent on a known, fixed set of goods are treated as "single-purpose vouchers" and can be taxed at the time of sale, under rules Shopify's own gift card system doesn't automatically apply for you.

Why merchants keep asking this

A gift card looks like a product in your Shopify admin — it has a price, it shows up in an order, and it appears in your sales reports. That makes it easy to assume it should be taxed like any other line item. But a gift card isn't a good or service on its own; it's closer to cash. Selling one just moves money from a customer's card to a stored balance they haven't spent yet.

The US answer: tax at redemption, not at purchase

In the US, the near-universal rule is that a gift card purchase itself isn't a taxable sale. Sales tax is charged on the actual goods or services the customer eventually buys with the card, at the time of that purchase. If you charged tax both when the card was sold and again when it was redeemed, the customer would effectively be taxed twice on the same money — which is exactly the confusion behind recurring Shopify Community threads asking whether gift cards are taxable at all. The consistent answer merchants land on is: don't charge tax on the card itself; let your normal checkout tax rules apply when it's spent.

The EU answer: it depends on what the card can buy

EU VAT rules split vouchers into two categories, and which one your gift card falls into changes when VAT is due:

  • Single-purpose vouchers (SPVs): if you know the VAT rate and country of supply at the time the card is issued — because it can only be redeemed for goods taxed at one specific rate, in one specific country — VAT is due at the point of sale, not redemption.
  • Multi-purpose vouchers (MPVs): if the card could be spent on goods or services taxed at different rates, or across borders, the VAT treatment isn't yet fixed at sale, so VAT is due at redemption instead — closer to the US model.

A general-purpose Shopify store gift card, redeemable storewide across a catalog that likely spans multiple VAT rates, usually behaves like an MPV in practice. But merchants selling a narrower catalog, or a card explicitly scoped to one product category taxed at one rate, can find themselves holding an SPV without realizing it — and Shopify's native gift card system doesn't ask you which one you're issuing or apply the right treatment automatically. That gap is a live, actively-discussed pain point in Shopify's own EU merchant community, not a settled feature.

A separate wrinkle: Shopify's own reporting can muddy this further

Merchants have also raised a related, unresolved complaint: Shopify's sales reporting counts gift cards as products and can double-count them as sales — once when the card is sold, and again when it's redeemed against an order. That doesn't change what tax you legally owe, but it does mean your revenue and tax reports can overstate real sales activity if you're reading them at face value without accounting for gift card issuance and redemption separately.

What this means for setting up your store

  • For most US-only stores, leave gift card purchases untaxed and rely on your existing checkout tax settings to apply tax correctly when a card is redeemed.
  • For EU sales, work out whether your card is genuinely single-purpose or multi-purpose before assuming Shopify is handling VAT timing correctly on your behalf — this is a tax-classification question, not something an app can decide for you.
  • Keep gift card issuance and redemption numbers separate when reconciling sales reports against what you actually owe in tax, since Shopify's own reporting doesn't cleanly separate the two.
  • This is general information, not tax advice — a merchant with EU sales or a narrow-catalog gift card program should confirm treatment with an accountant familiar with local VAT rules.

Where balance checking fits in

None of this changes what a shopper needs to know day to day: how much is left on their card. Frequently asked questions

Do I charge sales tax when someone buys a gift card on my Shopify store?

In the US, generally no — tax is applied when the card is redeemed for taxable goods, not when the card itself is purchased.

Does Shopify automatically handle EU VAT rules for gift cards?

Not reliably. Shopify's gift card feature doesn't ask whether a card is a single-purpose or multi-purpose voucher under EU VAT rules, so merchants selling into the EU need to work that classification out themselves rather than assume the platform applies it correctly.

Why does my gift card revenue look higher than my actual sales?

Shopify's reporting can count a gift card as a product sale when it's issued, then count it again when it's redeemed against an order — a known, unresolved reporting quirk that can inflate apparent sales if issuance and redemption aren't tracked separately.
