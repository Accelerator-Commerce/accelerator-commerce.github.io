Is Shopify Building Native Audio Support? Inside the "Shop Sounds" Hack Days Prototype

Short answer: Not yet, and not officially — but Shopify's own engineering team has already prototyped it. A Hack Days project called "Shop Sounds" added a new audios database table, a Merchandising::Audio model, and a Liquid media_tag that renders a built-in audio player for an uploaded file, the same way Shopify already renders 3D models today. It's a proof-of-concept, not a shipped feature or an announced roadmap item — but it's a real signal worth knowing about if you're evaluating an audio app for your store.

Where this comes from

Shopify Engineering's own blog documents internal Hack Days projects, and one recent writeup describes an engineer building "Shop Sounds" — a proof-of-concept for treating audio as a first-class type of product media, architected the same way Shopify already treats images, video, and 3D models. The article is explicit that "Shop Sounds isn't a live product today" and gives no shipping timeline. This is internal experimentation surfaced publicly, not a product announcement — the distinction matters for anyone deciding what to do based on it.

What Shop Sounds actually is, technically

  • A new audios database table. A dedicated storage layer for audio files as their own media type, rather than treating audio as a generic file attachment.
  • A Merchandising::Audio model with upload handling. Built to slot into Shopify's existing product-media pipeline alongside images, video, and 3D models.
  • A {{ product.media | media_tag }} Liquid tag that renders a built-in player. The same tag theme developers already use to render a 3D model viewer would, in this prototype, automatically render an audio player for an uploaded audio file — no theme-side audio-player code required.

In other words, the prototype treats an audio file the way Shopify already treats a product photo or a 3D model: upload it as media, and the storefront renders the right player automatically. That's architecturally different from how audio reaches a Shopify storefront today, which relies entirely on third-party apps injecting a player.

What it would (and wouldn't) replace

Even in its prototype form, Shop Sounds is scoped narrowly: it's a player for an audio file you've already uploaded as product media — a music sample, a voice memo, a sound effect. It says nothing about converting existing written content (a blog post, a product description) to speech. That's the core job a text-to-speech app like Listen Now does, and it's a fundamentally different problem: generating audio from text you already wrote, not playing back an audio file you upload separately. If Shop Sounds ever ships, it would most likely compete with (or reduce the need for) apps that exist purely to embed an uploaded-audio player — not with read-aloud/TTS functionality at all.

Why this is worth writing about now

No competitor or audio-app content currently mentions Shop Sounds — it's a fresh, primary-sourced signal straight from Shopify's own engineering team, not a rumor or a feature-request thread. A merchant who searches "will Shopify add native audio support" today finds nothing. That makes this a timely, defensible piece of content precisely because it's speculative: it explains what's known, what isn't, and why the answer doesn't change what a store needs right now.

What to do today

  • Don't wait on it. There's no shipping timeline, and Hack Days prototypes frequently never ship at all — that's the nature of an internal hackathon project.
  • Understand it wouldn't cover read-aloud content anyway. Even a shipped version of Shop Sounds addresses uploaded audio files as media, not converting your existing blog and product copy to speech — that's a separate problem a dedicated app still needs to solve.
  • Watch for an official announcement, not just this internal writeup. A Hack Days project graduating into an actual Shopify feature would typically show up in Shopify's changelog or a merchant-facing announcement first.

Where Listen Now fits

Listen Now solves the problem Shop Sounds doesn't touch: turning the blog posts and product descriptions you've already written into audio, automatically, without requiring you to record or upload a separate audio file for every piece of content. Even if Shopify eventually ships native support for playing an uploaded audio file, that's additive to — not a replacement for — a text-to-speech tool that generates audio from your existing written content.

Frequently asked questions

Is Shop Sounds an announced Shopify feature?

No. It's an internal Hack Days prototype documented on Shopify's engineering blog, explicitly described as not being a live product, with no announced release timeline.

Would Shop Sounds replace a text-to-speech app like Listen Now?

No. Shop Sounds, as prototyped, plays back an audio file you've already uploaded as product media — it doesn't convert written blog or product content into speech, which is what a TTS app actually does.

Should I hold off on installing an audio app while I wait to see what Shopify does?

There's no indication Shop Sounds is close to shipping, and even if it does, it solves a different problem than read-aloud text-to-speech. Waiting on it wouldn't get you the accessibility or content-engagement benefits a TTS app provides today.
