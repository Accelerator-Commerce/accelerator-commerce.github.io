Does Background Audio Hurt Accessibility on Shopify?

Short answer: Background music or narration itself isn't the accessibility problem — audio that starts playing automatically, without warning or a visible control to stop it, is. Shopify's own accessibility guidance is specific about this.

What Shopify's accessibility guidance actually says

Shopify's accessibility documentation for themes warns that unexpected or auto-playing audio can interfere with screen readers and disorient visitors who use assistive technology, and it states that controls to pause or stop audio should always be available. That guidance exists because screen reader users navigate by listening — audio that starts on its own competes directly with the assistive technology they depend on.

Autoplay is the risk, not audio itself

It's easy to read accessibility warnings about audio and conclude the safe move is to avoid audio features altogether. That's not what the guidance says. The problem is specifically audio a visitor didn't choose to start and can't easily stop — not audio in general. A player that stays silent until someone presses play creates none of the same risk.

What user-controlled audio looks like

  • Playback starts only when a visitor actively chooses it — never automatically on page load.
  • A visible, reachable control to pause or stop is present the entire time audio is playing.
  • The control works with a keyboard, not just a mouse or touch.
  • Volume or muting is easy to find, not hidden behind a settings menu.
  • The written content the audio represents stays on the page — audio is an option, not a replacement.

Using Listen Now the compliant way

Listen Now is built around manual playback: visitors see a player and choose to start it, rather than audio starting on its own. That lines up directly with Shopify's guidance — the player gives people who want background music, narration or text-to-speech a way to get it, without putting every other visitor, including screen reader users, at the mercy of audio they didn't ask for.

A quick self-check for existing audio features

  • Does anything on your storefront play sound before a visitor interacts with the page? If so, that's the fix to prioritize first.
  • Can a visitor stop audio using only a keyboard?
  • Is the play/pause control visible, or does a visitor have to hunt for it?

Frequently asked questions

Is any autoplay ever acceptable?

Treat autoplay as something to avoid on storefronts. Even short or muted autoplay can be unexpected for assistive technology users, and a visitor-initiated player removes the risk entirely without giving up the feature.

Does adding audio help or hurt SEO?

Audio itself is not a ranking factor. What matters for both SEO and accessibility is keeping the written content on the page — audio should be an added option, not a replacement for text search engines and screen readers can already read.
