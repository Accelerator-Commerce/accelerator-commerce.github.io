What "Free" Actually Means in Shopify Background Music Apps

Quick answer: "Free" background music apps typically limit the track library, add app branding to the player, cap uploads or storage, and hold back features like autoplay control or custom playlists for paid tiers. Check all four before assuming a free plan covers what you need.

"Best free" lists usually stop at "has a free tier"

Search for the best free Shopify background music app and most results are the same: a list of apps that happen to offer a free plan, with no detail on what that plan actually includes. That's a thin standard. Plenty of apps have a free tier that's free in name only once you look at what's actually gated behind an upgrade.

What to check before trusting a "free" plan

Four things separate a genuinely usable free plan from one that's free in name only:

1. The track library

A free plan's music library is almost always a subset of the full catalog. Before committing, check how many tracks the free tier actually includes and whether they're refreshed over time or static — a small, unchanging library gets repetitive fast on a storefront customers return to.

2. App branding on the player

Some free plans display the app's own name or logo on the player widget itself — a visible watermark on your storefront. That's a reasonable trade-off for a free tool, but it's a storefront-facing detail, not a backend setting, so it's worth knowing about upfront rather than noticing it after launch.

3. Upload and storage caps

If you plan to upload your own tracks or narration rather than using a stock library, check whether the free tier allows uploads at all, and if so, what the storage or file-count limit is. This is easy to miss because it doesn't matter until you actually try to add something beyond the default library.

4. Feature gates

The features that make a player usable day-to-day — control over autoplay behavior, building custom playlists, swapping music by season or page — are frequently reserved for paid plans even when the base player itself is free. A free plan that only offers one fixed playlist with no autoplay controls is a different product than one that lets you configure playback the way your store needs.

The question that matters most: what happens when you outgrow it

The most useful thing to check isn't what the free plan includes today — it's what happens to your existing setup if you need more later. If you add more tracks than the free tier allows, or want the app branding removed, does your current playlist and player configuration carry over cleanly into a paid plan, or do you start over? An app that makes upgrading disruptive costs you more than the subscription price.

Where Listen Now fits

Frequently asked questions

Is a free background music app good enough for a small store?

It can be, especially if a small, static track library and app branding on the player aren't dealbreakers for your storefront. The checklist above is how to confirm that before relying on it.

What's the biggest thing "best free app" lists leave out?

Whether upgrading later is smooth. Most lists compare what free plans include today and skip what happens to your setup — playlists, uploads, configuration — if you need to move to a paid plan afterward.
