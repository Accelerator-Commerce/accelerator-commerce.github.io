What Happens to a Gift Card's Balance in a Multi-Currency Shopify Store?

Short answer: A Shopify gift card is issued in one currency, and its balance is tracked and reported in that same issuance currency. Whether it can be redeemed for a purchase in a different currency depends on a setting the merchant chooses — either the card stays fixed to its original currency, or it converts at checkout using the store's exchange rate. A balance checker needs to show the right currency clearly, or it just adds to the confusion.

Why merchants keep asking about this

Two separate, unresolved threads in the Shopify Community ask essentially the same question: how do gift cards actually work for a store selling in multiple currencies? That it's been asked more than once, months apart, with no clear answer either time, says this isn't a one-off confusion — it's a real gap for any international store that sells gift cards.

How Shopify actually handles it

A few mechanics matter here, and they don't always match what a merchant expects by default:

  • A gift card is issued in a single currency — typically whatever currency the purchase that created it was made in. Its balance is held and reported in that currency, not automatically re-priced into whatever currency a shopper happens to be browsing in.
  • Redemption behavior is a merchant choice. Shopify lets a merchant decide whether a gift card is redeemable only in its original issuance currency (a fixed balance, no exchange-rate exposure) or convertible for use against a purchase in any currency the store sells in, using the store's live exchange rate at checkout.
  • These two settings produce very different shopper experiences. A fixed-currency card is simple but can feel restrictive to a shopper browsing in a different currency. A convertible card is more flexible but means the "balance" a shopper sees can shift slightly with exchange rates, which is worth explaining rather than leaving as a surprise.

What this means for balance checking specifically

A balance-check tool that just displays a number without stating a currency is ambiguous the moment a store sells in more than one. A shopper checking a card issued in EUR while browsing a USD-denominated storefront needs to know unambiguously which currency that number is in — and, if the card is convertible, ideally understand that the converted amount can shift with exchange rates rather than being a fixed guarantee.

For staff-facing lookups — at POS or in support — the same clarity matters even more, since a misread currency on a gift card balance is a direct, real-money mistake at checkout, not just a display quirk.

What a good multi-currency balance checker should do

  • Always label the currency next to the balance, not just the number.
  • Reflect the card's actual issuance currency, rather than silently converting it to the storefront's currently-selected currency.
  • Make fixed-vs-convertible behavior visible where the merchant has that setting enabled, so a shopper isn't caught off guard by a converted amount at checkout.
  • Work the same way at POS and online, so staff and shoppers aren't seeing two different, possibly conflicting, figures.

Frequently asked questions

Can a gift card's balance change on its own due to exchange rates?

No — the balance stored against the card doesn't fluctuate by itself. What can vary is how much of a purchase in a different currency that balance covers, if the merchant has enabled currency conversion at redemption.

Does this apply to both digital and physical gift cards?

Yes — the underlying currency and balance mechanics are the same regardless of how the card was delivered; what matters is which currency the card was issued in and how the merchant has configured redemption.

Should I choose fixed-currency or convertible gift cards for my store?

That depends on your customer base and how much cross-currency shopping you expect — a single-currency store has no need to think about this, while a store that regularly sees shoppers checking out in a different currency than a card's issuance currency should decide deliberately rather than leaving Shopify's default in place unexamined.
