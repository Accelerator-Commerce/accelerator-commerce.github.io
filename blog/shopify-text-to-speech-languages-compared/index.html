Shopify Text-to-Speech Apps Compared: Which Languages and Voices Do You Actually Get?

Quick answer: Language and voice counts on app listing pages are self-reported and unverified anywhere independently, and a big number doesn't guarantee good coverage of the specific languages your customers actually speak. Test real samples in your store's languages before choosing, rather than comparing headline numbers.

Every app reports a different big number

Look across Shopify text-to-speech apps and the marketing numbers don't line up with each other at all. One app advertises support for 75-plus languages. Another lists 60-plus AI voices spanning 35-plus languages and accents. A third builds its narration on a well-known third-party voice-synthesis engine and inherits whatever language list that provider ships. None of these figures come from an independent test — they're vendor-reported, and there's no published comparison anywhere that checks one against another under the same conditions.

That doesn't make the numbers meaningless, but it does mean they answer the wrong question. The number that matters isn't how many languages an app lists — it's how good the app sounds in the specific languages your customers use.

Why a big language count can be misleading

A few reasons a high total doesn't tell you what you need to know:

  • Voice quality varies a lot per language. An app's flagship voice — the one demoed on its listing page — is usually its best-supported language. Language number fifty on the list is rarely held to the same standard.
  • Not every language gets the same voice options. A store might get several voice choices in English and only one, less natural-sounding option in the language its actual customers speak.
  • Coverage isn't the same as correct pronunciation. A language being "supported" doesn't mean the narration will pronounce your brand name, product names or industry terms correctly — those are exactly the words generic language models are most likely to get wrong.

What to actually test before choosing

Rather than comparing language counts on app store listings, run through this before committing to any text-to-speech app:

  • Listen to a sample in the actual languages your customers use — not just the app's demo language, and not just English if your store sells internationally.
  • Check whether the voice can be set per store locale. If you run a multi-language storefront, confirm each language version can use its own matched voice rather than defaulting to one narrator across every locale.
  • Test your own brand and product names. Run a real product title or brand term through the narration and listen for mispronunciation — this is the detail vendor demo clips are least likely to expose.
  • Check that non-English narration isn't a lesser-tier feature. Some pricing plans gate the fuller voice or language list behind a higher tier — confirm what you get at the plan level you'd actually use.

Where Listen Now fits for international stores

Frequently asked questions

Should I just pick the app with the highest language count?

No. A high total tells you almost nothing about whether the languages your actual customers speak sound good. Test the specific languages you need instead of comparing headline numbers.

Is a third-party voice engine better than an app's own voices?

Not automatically. What matters is how the specific voice sounds in your language with your content, not whether the underlying technology has a recognizable name.

Can I use different voices for different store languages?

With Listen Now, yes — voices can be matched to your store's locales rather than using one voice across every language version of your storefront.
