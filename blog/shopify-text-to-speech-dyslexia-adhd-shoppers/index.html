Read-Aloud Shopping: How Text-to-Speech Helps Dyslexic and ADHD Customers Browse Your Store

Short answer: Text-to-speech gets talked about almost entirely as an ADA compliance item for blind and low-vision visitors, but a much larger group of shoppers — people with dyslexia or ADHD — also read more slowly, lose their place, or disengage from dense written content, without needing a screen reader or any assistive technology at all. A visible, optional "listen to this" button turns your product descriptions and blog content into something they can follow by ear instead of losing patience with the page and leaving.

Reading online is harder for more shoppers than "accessibility" checklists account for

Most Shopify accessibility content — including our own — frames text-to-speech around blind and low-vision visitors and legal compliance under WCAG and the ADA. That framing is accurate as far as it goes, but it leaves out a much bigger, mostly invisible group: shoppers with dyslexia, ADHD, or other reading-processing differences who can see the page just fine but find long blocks of text genuinely tiring to get through. Dyslexia alone is estimated to affect a meaningful share of the general population, and unlike a vision impairment, it's rarely disclosed or obvious to a merchant — these shoppers don't show up in your accessibility audit, but they're on your product pages every day.

The demand for this is well documented — just not yet on Shopify's own forums

General assistive-technology communities built around ADHD and dyslexia run long-running, active discussions recommending text-to-speech tools for exactly this reason — Speechify in particular comes up constantly in that context as a tool people with ADHD and dyslexia use to get through reading they'd otherwise avoid or abandon. That's a real, sustained, non-Shopify- specific signal. What doesn't exist yet is a Shopify Community thread specifically asking for dyslexia- or ADHD-oriented read-aloud shopping — which makes this an unclaimed content and product-positioning angle rather than a live, urgent support request. The need is real; the audience just hasn't framed it as a Shopify feature request yet.

Where read-aloud helps most on a storefront

  • Long product descriptions. Detailed spec lists, ingredient breakdowns, or sizing and care instructions are exactly the kind of dense text that's easy to skim past — and easy to miss important details in — without ever fully reading.
  • Blog and guide content. Buying guides, comparison posts, and how-to content tend to run long, which is where reading fatigue sets in hardest for a dyslexic or easily-distracted reader.
  • FAQs and policy pages. Return policies, shipping details and size guides are important enough that you want every shopper to actually absorb them, not skim and guess.

This is a UX and conversion feature, not just a legal checkbox

Framing text-to-speech purely as an ADA risk-mitigation item — which is a real and separate reason to have it, covered in our ADA audio accessibility checklist — undersells what it actually does for a much larger slice of your traffic. A shopper who finds a product description tedious to read doesn't file an accessibility complaint; they just leave the tab open, get distracted, and don't come back. An audio option doesn't just reduce legal exposure — it can be the difference between a shopper finishing a product description and bouncing halfway through it.

How Listen Now fits

Listen Now adds a visible, visitor-controlled audio option to Shopify content — product pages, collection pages and blog posts — so a shopper who'd rather listen than read can do that without losing anything: the written text stays exactly where it was, and the audio is purely an additional way to consume the same content. That optional, side-by-side setup is what makes it useful for a dyslexic or ADHD shopper specifically — nothing about the page changes for anyone who doesn't use it, and the choice is entirely theirs.

Frequently asked questions

Is this different from adding text-to-speech for ADA compliance?

The underlying feature is the same — a visible, optional audio player — but the reasoning is different. ADA compliance is about legal risk tied to a documented category of visitors. This is about a much larger, largely invisible group of shoppers who benefit from an audio option purely for reading ease, whether or not a compliance requirement applies to your store at all.

Do I need to specifically market this as a "dyslexia" or "ADHD" feature?

Not necessarily — a simple, visible "listen to this page" option works for anyone who prefers audio, without requiring a shopper to identify why they want it. Naming the use case in your own marketing or FAQ content can still help it get found by the people actively looking for it.

Is there Shopify-specific demand data for this yet?

Not in the form of a live Shopify Community thread as of this writing — the demand signal currently comes from general assistive-technology communities, not Shopify-specific forums. It's a real, well-documented need that hasn't yet been articulated as a Shopify feature request.
