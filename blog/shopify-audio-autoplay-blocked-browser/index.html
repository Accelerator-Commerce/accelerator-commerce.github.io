Why Won't My Shopify Store's Background Music or TTS Play Automatically?

Quick answer: This is almost never an app bug — every major browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari) blocks audio with sound from autoplaying until a visitor interacts with the page first, usually with a click or tap. It's a deliberate browser policy meant to stop unwanted sound from ambushing visitors, and it applies to background music and text-to-speech players exactly the same way it applies to video.

This is a browser policy, not a broken install

Autoplay-with-sound restrictions rolled out across major browsers years ago specifically to stop sites from blasting audio the instant a page loads, and they've only gotten stricter since. Every mainstream browser now requires some form of user "engagement" with a page — most commonly a click, tap, or scroll — before it will allow audio with sound to start playing on its own. Muted autoplay is generally still allowed, which is exactly why autoplaying background video with no sound works while autoplaying music with sound usually doesn't.

Why this gets mistaken for an app problem

Existing coverage of autoplay restrictions is almost entirely written around video backgrounds — hero banners, product videos — not around dedicated audio apps like background-music players or text-to-speech readers. That gap means merchants troubleshooting a silent audio player often can't find an explanation that actually matches their situation, and end up assuming the app itself is misconfigured or broken when the browser is the one blocking it, on purpose, by design.

What actually determines whether audio can autoplay

  • Whether the visitor has interacted with the page yet. A single click or tap anywhere on the page is often enough to "unlock" audio for the rest of that visit.
  • Whether the audio is muted. Muted autoplay is broadly permitted; unmuted autoplay is the part that gets blocked.
  • Browser-specific engagement scoring. Chrome in particular tracks a per-visitor "media engagement" score based on past interaction with audio/video on a site, which can allow autoplay for returning visitors even without a fresh click.
  • Autoplay isn't the same as "won't play at all." A player blocked from autoplaying will still work normally once a visitor taps play — the restriction only affects the automatic, unprompted start.

What to design around instead of fighting

Trying to force autoplay past a browser's own policy isn't a fight worth having — it's a deliberate restriction the browser vendors aren't going to relax. The more reliable approach is designing the audio experience to expect a first interaction: a visible, obvious play button rather than a hidden autoplay attempt, and clear visual cues (like a pulsing icon or a prompt) that invite a visitor to start the audio themselves. This also happens to be better UX regardless of the technical restriction, since visitors who didn't expect sound and get ambushed by it tend to bounce immediately.

Where Listen Now fits

Listen Now's players are built around this reality rather than fighting it — a clear, visible play control that works the moment a visitor taps it, instead of relying on an autoplay behavior that modern browsers won't reliably allow anyway. If your store's background music or text-to-speech audio isn't starting on page load, that's very likely expected browser behavior, not something that needs fixing on the app side.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a way to force audio to autoplay with sound in every browser?

Not reliably, and not in a way worth pursuing — this is a deliberate, actively maintained browser policy across Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and Safari, not a workaround-able bug. Design for a visitor-initiated start instead.

Does muted autoplay still work for background music or TTS?

Muted autoplay is generally permitted, but it defeats the purpose for background music or spoken audio, since the whole point is for the visitor to hear it. A visible play button that unmutes on click is the practical alternative.

Why does audio sometimes autoplay for returning visitors but not new ones?

Some browsers, notably Chrome, track a per-visitor engagement score based on past interaction with media on a site, which can allow autoplay for visitors who've previously interacted with audio there — even without a fresh click on that specific visit.
