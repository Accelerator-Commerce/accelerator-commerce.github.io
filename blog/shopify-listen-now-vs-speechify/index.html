Listen Now! vs. Speechify for Shopify: Which Text-to-Speech App Actually Fits Your Store?

Quick answer: Speechify Voice & Search reads everything available on a page, broadly and generically. Listen Now reads the content you choose to turn into audio — your blog posts and product copy — as a focused, branded listening experience. Pick based on what you actually want narrated, not just by comparing feature lists.

Two apps that show up in the same searches

Search for a Shopify text-to-speech app and both Listen Now and Speechify Voice & Search can turn up in the results. That makes them look like direct competitors, but they're built around different jobs. Knowing which job you actually need done matters more than which app has more reviews or a longer feature list.

What Speechify does

Speechify Voice & Search is a broad, page-wide reader — it's built to speak essentially every piece of text available on a page, not a curated subset a merchant selects. It's priced starting at $7.99 a month, and as of this comparison carries only a handful of reviews, which is worth keeping in mind: a low review count on a newer listing isn't a red flag by itself, but it does mean there's less of a track record to judge against than an app that's been running on many stores for longer.

What Listen Now does

Listen Now is narrower by design: it takes a blog article or a piece of product copy a merchant has written and turns that specific content into audio, plus background text-to-speech playback. It isn't trying to read an entire page end to end — it's built around the content a merchant actually wants a shopper to be able to listen to, priced at $3–5 a month with a free trial. If you're thinking about text-to-speech as an accessibility and content-consumption feature for specific articles or product descriptions rather than a universal page reader, that's the model Listen Now is built around.

Why the difference matters when you're choosing

  • Scope. A whole-page reader covers navigation text, footers, and everything else on a page along with your actual content. A content-specific reader only voices what you've chosen — your blog posts, your product descriptions.
  • Control over the experience. A narrower, content-specific player is easier to brand and keep consistent, since you're not relying on a generic page-reading engine to decide what gets read and in what order.
  • Price and maturity. A broader, more general-purpose reader tends to carry a higher price point; a focused content-reader app can be priced lower because the job it does is narrower. Review count and time on the market are also worth weighing alongside price.

This is a different distinction than "AI-generated audio"

Don't confuse this comparison with the separate question of apps that generate brand-new marketing copy with AI and then voice that generated text, rather than reading content you already wrote. See our breakdown of AI-generated product audio vs. reading your existing content aloud for that distinction — Speechify and Listen Now are both read-your-existing-content tools; the generation-vs-narration question is a separate axis entirely.

Which one fits your store

If you want a general-purpose reader that speaks whatever's on a page without much curation, a broad page-wide tool like Speechify is built for that. If you want a focused, branded audio experience for the specific blog content and product copy you've already invested in writing — without paying for, or exposing shoppers to, a reader that also speaks everything else on the page — that's the narrower model Listen Now is built around. For a fuller buyer's checklist covering what to look for across the category generally, see our guide to picking a Shopify text-to-speech app.

Frequently asked questions

Does a broader page-reader do more for the same price?

Not necessarily — "broader" means it reads more of the page, not that it does more for your specific content. If what you actually want is your blog posts and product descriptions read well and consistently, a focused tool can do that job just as effectively, often at a lower price.

Should I avoid an app with only a few reviews?

Not automatically. A low review count usually just means the app or listing is newer, not that it's unreliable. It's one data point worth weighing alongside price, scope, and a free trial — not a disqualifier by itself.

Can I use both types of app on the same store?

Technically yes, but running two overlapping read-aloud tools on the same page is more likely to create a confusing, duplicated experience for shoppers than to add value. Pick the one that matches what you actually want narrated.
