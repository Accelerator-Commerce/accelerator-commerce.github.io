What Happens When a Shopify Gift Card Expires?

Quick answer: What happens to an expired gift card's balance depends heavily on state law, not just store policy. The federal CARD Act sets a five-year minimum before a gift card can expire and limits inactivity fees, but individual states go further — California doesn't allow gift cards to expire at all, while states with escheatment (unclaimed property) laws can require unredeemed balances to eventually be turned over to the state rather than kept by the merchant. What a shopper sees when checking an old card's balance depends on where the purchase happened and what the merchant's own policy allows under that state's rules.

The federal floor: the CARD Act

The Credit CARD Act of 2009 sets a national baseline for gift card expiration: a card can't expire less than five years after it was issued or last loaded with funds, and any inactivity fees have to be clearly disclosed and limited. That's the floor every U.S. merchant has to meet — but it's a floor, not a ceiling, and plenty of states layer stricter rules on top of it.

Why the same card can behave differently in different states

State law is where most of the real variation lives, and it's substantial:

  • California doesn't allow gift cards to expire, period — no five-year window, no exceptions for most retail gift cards, full stop.
  • New York treats the entire unredeemed balance of an expired card as abandoned property once the relevant dormancy period passes, meaning the state — not the merchant — is legally entitled to claim it.
  • North Carolina only claims 60% of an expired balance as abandoned property, leaving the remainder in a different legal position than New York's full-balance approach.
  • Escheatment (unclaimed property) law is the mechanism behind all of this — it's the same legal framework that requires banks to turn over dormant accounts to the state, applied to gift card balances that go unredeemed long enough.

Merchants themselves are confused about this — and so are shoppers

This isn't a hypothetical concern. Shopify merchants have publicly asked what actually happens to a gift card's balance and order record once it expires, asked the same question again from a pure accounting perspective, and asked how to configure a fixed expiry date on gift cards in the first place — all signs that expiration mechanics aren't clearly understood even by the people setting the policy, let alone the shoppers checking an old card months or years later.

What a shopper checking an old card's balance should actually expect

  • If the card is less than five years old, it should still work everywhere — the CARD Act's federal minimum applies regardless of state.
  • If the purchase happened in a state like California, expiration may not be a factor at all, even for a very old card.
  • If a balance shows as zero or missing well past a reasonable timeframe, escheatment to the state is a real possibility in states with aggressive unclaimed-property rules — the balance isn't necessarily "gone," it may have been legally transferred rather than forfeited.
  • Store policy still matters within whatever the state allows — the state sets the outer boundary, but a merchant's own gift card terms determine the specifics inside it.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Shopify gift card ever legally expire less than five years after purchase?

No, not under U.S. federal law — the CARD Act sets five years as the minimum, and some states extend that further or prohibit expiration entirely. Any card expiring sooner than five years wouldn't be compliant with the federal floor.

If my gift card balance shows as zero, was it stolen or forfeited?

Not necessarily — in states with escheatment laws, an unredeemed balance can be legally transferred to the state as abandoned property after a dormancy period, which is a different outcome than the merchant simply keeping or losing the funds. Checking directly with the merchant or state unclaimed-property office is the way to confirm what happened to a specific balance.

Does gift card expiration law vary by state even for the same merchant?

Yes — a merchant selling nationally generally has to apply the strictest applicable rule for each state a card was issued in, which is why the same merchant's gift cards can behave differently depending on where the purchase happened.
