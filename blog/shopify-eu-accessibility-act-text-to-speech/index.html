The EU Accessibility Act Is Already Being Enforced — Does Your Shopify Store's Audio Experience Count?

Short answer: Yes, if you sell into the EU. The European Accessibility Act (EAA) became enforceable on June 28, 2025 across all 27 member states, ties compliance to EN 301 549 and WCAG 2.1 Level AA, and carries fines of up to €500,000. Several compliance guides list text-to-speech as a relevant accessibility capability — but before any TTS tool can read your product or blog content aloud, that content has to actually exist as text, not be baked into an image.

This isn't a future risk — it's already in force

Unlike the ADA lawsuit wave merchants hear about as an ongoing US legal risk, the EAA is a different kind of deadline: a specific date that has already passed. It became enforceable across the European Union on June 28, 2025, and enforcement bodies are reported to be "scaling up audits through 2026." If your Shopify store sells to customers in the EU — even as a secondary market alongside a US-focused business — this isn't a hypothetical compliance project to get to eventually. It's a standard that's already being checked against.

What the EAA actually requires

The EAA ties compliance to EN 301 549, the EU's technical accessibility standard, which in turn maps closely to WCAG 2.1 Level AA — the same web content accessibility guidelines that US ADA compliance guidance leans on. In practice, that means a store needs to be perceivable and operable for people with visual, auditory, motor, and cognitive disabilities: sufficient color contrast, keyboard navigability, properly labeled form fields and images, and — relevant here — ways for content to be consumed by someone who can't easily read it on screen. Several compliance guides written for ecommerce merchants explicitly name text-to-speech as one of the capabilities that supports this requirement for a visually-impaired shopper.

Real merchants are already asking what's left for them to handle

A live Shopify Community thread, EU Accessibility Act 2025 Compliance, shows merchants asking exactly this question: what is Shopify itself handling at the platform level, and what's left for the store owner to configure — theme customization, checkout, and third-party apps. That's a fair question, and the honest answer is that Shopify's platform-level accessibility work doesn't cover everything a specific store adds on top of it, including whether its own content is actually readable by assistive technology or convertible to audio.

The gap most stores miss first: text that isn't really text

Before text-to-speech can read anything aloud, the content has to exist as real, selectable text — not as words baked into a product photo, a size-guide graphic, or a care-instruction image. A TTS tool, a screen reader, and a browser's own translation feature all share this same limitation: none of them can read text trapped inside a picture. A surprising amount of product detail — fabric composition, wash instructions, fit notes — ends up rendered as an image because it was easier to design that way, which quietly locks that content out of any accessibility tool, TTS included, before the tool even gets a chance to work. Auditing product and policy pages for this is a low-effort, high-leverage first step toward EAA readiness that has nothing to do with installing any particular app.

How this is different from the ADA angle

If you've already looked at our ADA audio accessibility lawsuit checklist, that piece is US-focused — built around digital accessibility lawsuit exposure and demand letters under the Americans with Disabilities Act. The EAA is a separate, EU-specific legal framework with its own enforcement date, its own named technical standard, and its own fine structure. A store selling into both markets has two distinct compliance questions to answer, not one — and this is the EU half.

Frequently asked questions

Does the EAA apply to a Shopify store based outside the EU?

If you sell products or services to customers located in the EU, the EAA's requirements can apply regardless of where your business is legally based — it's about who you're selling to, not just where you're incorporated. A qualified compliance professional can confirm how it applies to your specific situation.

Does installing a text-to-speech app make a store EAA-compliant?

No single app makes a store fully compliant. The EAA covers the whole shopping experience — contrast, navigation, forms, and more — not just audio. A TTS player addresses one specific capability several compliance guides call out, as part of a broader accessibility effort.

What's the first thing to check before adding text-to-speech for EAA purposes?

Confirm the content you want read aloud — product details, size guides, care instructions — is actual selectable text rather than text baked into an image. No TTS tool can read what isn't there as text in the first place.
