Chrome Can Already Read Your Store Aloud for Free — Here's What a Dedicated TTS App Still Gets You

Short answer: Yes — Chrome for Android has a built-in "Listen to this page" feature that reads any page aloud, including a Shopify blog post or product page, with no app install. It's a real objection worth answering honestly, not ignoring. What it doesn't cover is everyone who isn't on Chrome for Android, a branded in-page player, or voice and playback consistency you control as a merchant.

What Chrome's native feature actually does

Since Chrome 121, Android's version of Chrome has shipped a "Listen to this page" option in the three-dot browser menu, sitting between "Translate" and "Add to Home screen." Tap it and Chrome reads the current page aloud using an on-device or cloud text-to-speech voice, with a full miniplayer: play/pause, a 10-second scrub control, a voice picker, and playback speeds from 0.5x to 4x. It works on more or less any page with readable text — a news article, a blog post, a Shopify product description — and it costs nothing and requires nothing from the site owner. That's a genuine, current, free alternative to installing a dedicated audio app, and it's worth naming directly rather than hoping shoppers don't notice it.

Why this is worth addressing head-on

The honest version of "why would I pay for an audio app when the browser already does this?" is a fair question, and it deserves a fair answer instead of being ignored. A merchant evaluating Listen Now or any other Shopify audio app is entitled to know exactly what a generic, system-level reader does and doesn't do compared to a purpose-built, in-page player designed around a specific store's content.

What Chrome's built-in reader doesn't cover

  • It's Chrome-for-Android only. A shopper on desktop Safari, desktop Firefox, desktop Chrome, or an iPhone doesn't get this feature at all — and a meaningful share of any store's traffic falls outside Chrome on Android specifically.
  • It's a system overlay, not a branded player. The miniplayer looks and behaves the same on every site a visitor uses it on. It carries no store branding, sits outside the page's own design, and gives a merchant no control over how or where the play control appears.
  • Voice quality and consistency aren't a merchant's choice. The voice a shopper hears depends on whatever voice they've picked (or left on default) in their own Chrome settings — not a voice the merchant selected to match their brand, and not guaranteed to sound the same from one shopper's device to the next.
  • It reads the whole page indiscriminately. A generic page reader doesn't know the difference between a product description worth narrating and a footer full of navigation links, policy text, and boilerplate — it just reads what's on the page in document order.
  • There's no player embedded for a shopper to discover. A visitor has to already know the feature exists and go dig it out of Chrome's menu. A play button built into the page itself, next to the content it narrates, gets found and used by people who've never heard of a browser-level reading tool.
  • Nothing ties it to analytics or SEO signals. A merchant gets no visibility into how many shoppers used it, on which pages, for how long — a dedicated player that logs engagement gives a merchant something to act on that a system-level tool never will.

Where Listen Now still earns its place

Listen Now puts a branded, in-page audio player directly on a blog post or product page — visible to every visitor regardless of device or browser, not dependent on a shopper already knowing a hidden system menu exists. It uses a consistent, chosen voice across a whole catalog rather than whatever a visitor's own device happens to default to, and because it's built for Shopify specifically, it can be scoped to the content that's actually worth narrating rather than reading an entire page front to back. None of that competes with what Chrome's native reader does for a shopper who already knows to look for it — it's built for the much larger set of visitors who don't.

The honest way to frame this to a merchant considering both

A dedicated audio app isn't trying to out-compete a free, built-in browser feature at the thing that feature already does well for the narrow slice of visitors who use it. It's solving the much larger problem of reaching everyone else, with a player that's visible, on-brand, and consistent, instead of leaving audio access to whether a shopper happens to know their own phone's settings menu.

Frequently asked questions

Does Chrome's "Listen to this page" feature work on desktop?

No — as of this writing it's specific to Chrome for Android. Desktop Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Edge don't have an equivalent built-in page-reading feature, which is a meaningful gap for any store with desktop traffic.

Should a merchant worry this makes a TTS app redundant?

Not really — it's a real alternative for a narrow slice of visitors (Chrome on Android, who already know the feature exists), not a substitute for a branded, discoverable, cross-device player built into the page itself.

Can Listen Now and Chrome's native reader coexist on the same page?

Yes — they're not mutually exclusive. A shopper could technically use either one; Listen Now just makes the option visible and consistent instead of hidden behind a browser menu only some visitors know to check.
