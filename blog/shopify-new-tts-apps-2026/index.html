Two New AI Text-to-Speech Apps Launched on Shopify in 2026 — How Does Listen Now Stack Up?

Quick answer: Voiceify (January 2026) and EcomAudio (September 2026) both entered the Shopify text-to-speech category this year, each with a different pitch — broad language coverage, and a hybrid AI-voice-or-upload-your-own-audio model with built-in analytics. Neither changes what Listen Now is built for: turning the blog content and product copy you've already written into a focused, branded listening experience, not generating new text or trying to be the broadest player in the category.

The Shopify text-to-speech category got more crowded this year

Two new entrants launched on Shopify's app ecosystem in 2026, and it's worth knowing what each one actually does before you assume "newer" means "better fit for your store." A bigger category is good for merchants — more competition usually means sharper pricing and faster feature iteration — but it also means more marketing claims to sort through.

Voiceify: the broad-language-coverage entrant

Voiceify launched in January 2026 and leads with scale — it advertises support for 75-plus languages, a number aimed squarely at merchants selling internationally. As we've covered separately, a big language count is a self-reported marketing figure, not an independently verified one, and it doesn't guarantee the specific language your customers speak sounds natural. See our comparison of what Shopify text-to-speech apps actually deliver by language for how to evaluate that kind of claim properly — test real samples in the languages that matter to your store rather than comparing headline totals.

EcomAudio: upload-your-own audio, plus built-in analytics

EcomAudio launched on September 10, 2026, per its own app listing, with a different pitch entirely. Rather than relying only on AI-generated narration, it offers a hybrid model: six AI voices to choose from, or the option to upload your own pre-recorded MP3 or M4A file instead — a genuinely different approach from every other Shopify TTS competitor we've tracked, all of which generate narration from text with no manual-upload path. It starts at $4.99 a month on a Starter plan, and ships with play-count analytics built in, directly answering a question we'd previously flagged as unanswered in the category: whether any Shopify audio app reports how far customers actually listen in. As a brand-new listing, it carries no reviews yet, so there's no track record to weigh against the feature list — worth factoring in alongside the features themselves.

Where Listen Now fits against both

Listen Now isn't trying to win on language-count scale like Voiceify, or on a bring-your-own-audio hybrid model like EcomAudio. It's built around a narrower, specific job: taking the blog articles and product copy a merchant has already written and turning that exact content into a focused, branded audio experience, with background text-to-speech playback included. That's a different design choice than either new entrant, not a gap — the question worth asking isn't which app has the longest feature list, it's which model matches what you actually want narrated on your store.

  • If your priority is reaching international shoppers in their own language, Voiceify's language breadth is the relevant claim to test — in your actual target languages, not from the listing page.
  • If you want to mix AI narration with professionally recorded audio you already own, or you want listen-count reporting out of the box, EcomAudio's hybrid model is worth a look, with the caveat that it's unreviewed and new to the market.
  • If what you want is your existing blog and product content read well, consistently, and under your own brand, that's the job Listen Now is built around.

This is also a different question entirely from apps that generate brand-new marketing text with AI and then voice that generated copy, rather than reading content you wrote. Frequently asked questions

Should I switch apps just because something newer launched?

Not automatically. A new launch is worth knowing about, but it isn't a reason to switch on its own — weigh the actual feature you'd gain (broader language coverage, bring-your-own-audio, built-in analytics) against the track record and price of what you're already running.

Is a 75-plus-language claim something I can rely on?

Treat it as a starting point, not a guarantee. Test the specific language your customers speak before committing — vendor-reported totals don't tell you how natural any one language actually sounds.

Does built-in play-count analytics matter if I don't have it today?

It's a genuinely useful feature if you want to know how far people listen, but it isn't essential to get value from a text-to-speech app. Decide based on whether you'd actually act on that data, not just whether a competitor lists it.
