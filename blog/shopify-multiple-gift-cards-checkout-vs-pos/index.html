Why Can't I Use Two Gift Cards on One Shopify Order? Checkout vs. POS, Explained

Short answer: Shopify's online checkout is built to accept more than one gift card code per order, but a longstanding interface issue hides the second code field after the first card is applied, which makes it look unsupported even when it isn't. Shopify POS is more restrictive by design — POS Pro only allows one gift card to be redeemed per transaction, full stop. The two channels behave differently, and nothing in either checkout flow explains that to the person standing there holding two cards.

Online checkout: it should work, but the second field goes missing

On a standard Shopify online checkout, gift cards are entered as a code in a dedicated field, and the platform is designed to let a shopper apply more than one to the same order. In practice, multiple Shopify Community reports describe the same confusing symptom: after the first gift card code is entered and applied, the field to enter a second code simply isn't there anymore, or isn't obviously there. Shoppers and merchants have separately asked whether a second card can be applied at all, because the checkout doesn't make it clear how — or whether it's even possible in that moment.

The underlying capability is real. The confusion is a discoverability problem, not a missing feature: the interface doesn't surface an obvious "add another gift card" affordance once one is already applied, so plenty of shoppers assume the answer is no and give up.

Shopify POS: one gift card, one transaction, no exceptions

In person, the rules are simpler and stricter. Shopify POS Pro currently allows exactly one gift card to be redeemed per transaction — not a display bug, not a setting to toggle, just a hard limit on how many gift cards a single sale can apply. A customer at the register with two gift cards and a purchase that exceeds either card's balance can't simply hand over both and have the difference split between them in one transaction.

This is a real, live pain point for staff and customers alike, and it's a completely separate limitation from the online checkout confusion above — a merchant who's only ever sold online might not know POS behaves differently at all, and vice versa.

What to actually do if you have two gift cards

  • Online: apply the first gift card code, then check the order summary — some themes and checkout versions do still surface a way to add a second code, even if it isn't obvious. If a second field genuinely never appears, the order will need to be split, or the remaining balance covered by another payment method.
  • At a physical register (POS): since only one gift card can be redeemed per transaction, staff generally need to either split the purchase into two separate transactions — one per card — or apply one gift card and cover the rest with another payment method.
  • Either way, check both balances first. Knowing exactly what's left on each card before you're at checkout avoids the awkward moment of discovering a shortfall mid-sale — see our guide on checking a gift card balance safely online.

Why staff need to know this before a customer does

A shopper holding two gift cards at checkout has already decided how they want to pay — the worst time to discover a platform limitation is in front of them, mid-transaction, with a line forming. Staff who know in advance that POS caps out at one gift card per sale can set expectations before ringing anything up, rather than troubleshooting live. The same applies online: a support team that knows about the missing second-field issue can proactively explain the workaround instead of escalating what looks like a broken checkout.

Where a balance checker fits in

None of this is really about balance checking on its own, but it's the step that makes the rest of it manageable. A customer who already knows both card balances before they start checking out — rather than finding out mid-transaction — can decide in advance whether to split the purchase, which card to use first, or whether one card alone will cover it. Gift Check Pro is built for exactly that: a fast, self-serve way to check a balance before it becomes a checkout-line problem.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a bug that Shopify is going to fix?

The online checkout issue behaves like a UX/discoverability gap rather than a documented, fixed limitation — Community reports describe it, but there's no confirmed timeline for a change. The POS one-gift-card-per-transaction limit is a stated behavior of POS Pro, not a bug, so it shouldn't be expected to change without an explicit platform update.

Can a customer use a gift card and a discount code together?

Yes — gift cards and discount codes are handled separately by Shopify's checkout and generally don't conflict with each other. The limitation covered here is specifically about combining multiple gift cards, not about combining a gift card with a discount.

Does this affect Shopify Plus stores differently?

The core platform behavior is the same across plans — the gap is in the standard checkout and POS flows, not something tied to a specific plan tier. A Plus merchant with a heavily customized checkout could theoretically change the presentation, but the underlying POS transaction limit isn't something checkout customization affects.

What should staff do if a customer insists on using both cards in one sale at POS?

Since POS Pro only redeems one gift card per transaction, the practical options are splitting the purchase into two transactions (one per card) or applying one card and using a different payment method for the remainder — there isn't a setting that allows both in a single sale.
