Before You Install a Shopify Gift Card App, Check the Pricing Fine Print

Short answer: Gift card apps sit on top of real customer balances and purchase history, so a pricing change isn't just an inconvenience the way it might be for a cosmetic app — it can mean losing access to data your shoppers are counting on. Before installing one, check what happens to your data and your storefront if the app's free plan changes or you decide to leave.

A real pattern worth knowing about

Public app-store reviews for gift card and store-credit apps in this category show a recurring complaint pattern: a merchant builds on a free plan, the vendor later restructures that plan into a paid tier, and the merchant finds their storefront feature — or their own access to their own customer data — blocked until they accept the new pricing, sometimes with only a discounted "stay" offer presented as the way out. Some reviewers describe paying just to export their own data before switching apps entirely.

This isn't a generic "SaaS pricing changes" complaint. It's specific to apps that hold something a merchant can't easily walk away from — customer gift card balances, store credit, redemption history — which is exactly what makes a pricing change in this category higher-stakes than in most.

Why gift card apps are a special case

Most Shopify apps, if you uninstall them, just stop doing whatever cosmetic or convenience thing they did. A gift card or balance-checking app is different: the data it manages represents real money your shoppers are owed. If a vendor restructures pricing and you can't or won't pay, the practical question isn't "do I lose a feature" — it's "can my customers still find out what they're owed, and can I still see it myself."

Questions worth asking before you install

  • Is the underlying data mine? If gift card balances and history live as standard Shopify data rather than something the app holds hostage in its own system, you keep access to it regardless of what happens to the app relationship.
  • What happens on uninstall? Ask — or check reviews for — whether removing the app removes only its storefront widget, or breaks something a customer depends on.
  • Has the vendor changed its pricing structure before? A search of the app's own review page for words like "pricing," "blocked," or "locked" surfaces this kind of pattern faster than reading the pricing page alone.
  • Is there a free plan with real functionality, or a free trial with a hard cutoff? A plan structured to expand your usage before restricting it is a different risk profile than a flat, disclosed price from day one.
  • Can you export your data on your own, at any time, without needing to accept an offer or contact support first?

What to look for instead

A gift card app that's transparent about pricing from the start — a flat, disclosed cost rather than a free tier designed to convert later — gives you a predictable answer to "what happens if pricing changes" before you've built anything on top of it. The safest structural choice is an app that reads and displays standard Shopify gift card data rather than migrating it into a separate, proprietary system you'd need to extract later.

Where Gift Check Pro fits

Frequently asked questions

How do I check a gift card app's pricing history before installing it?

Read the app's Shopify App Store reviews, sorted by most recent, and specifically look for mentions of pricing changes, plan restructuring, or access being restricted — merchants who've been through this are usually vocal about it in reviews.

Is a free-forever plan always safer than a paid one?

Not necessarily — a "free" plan with no clear path to sustainability is sometimes the one most likely to be restructured later. A modest, disclosed, flat price can be a more predictable long-term commitment than an unsustainable free tier.

What's the single most important thing to check?

Whether the app works with Shopify's native gift card data or migrates it into a separate system. That one distinction determines whether you keep access to customer balances if you ever switch apps.
