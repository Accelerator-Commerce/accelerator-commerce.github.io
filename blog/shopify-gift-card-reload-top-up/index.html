Can You Reload a Shopify Gift Card? What Merchants Keep Asking (and What Actually Works)

Short answer: No — Shopify has no native way to add value to an existing gift card. Every reload request has to become a brand-new gift card with its own code. That's a genuine limitation, not a setup mistake, and it's the single most-asked gift card question in Shopify's own Community forums.

A question that keeps coming back

Search the Shopify Community for "reload gift card" or "top up gift card" and you'll find the same question asked, independently, over and over across years of threads — merchants running loyalty programs, return-credit workflows, or repeat-customer perks who assume there's a button somewhere to add funds to a card a customer already has. There isn't. Shopify's gift card system is built around fixed-value cards: once issued, a card's balance only goes down through redemption, never up through a top-up.

Why this isn't just an oversight

Shopify gift cards are simple by design — a code, a starting balance, and a running total as it's spent. That simplicity is what makes them reliable and easy to reconcile, but it also means the system has no concept of "add more value to this same code." Every native "reload" is actually a second, independent gift card, which is why merchants keep hitting the same wall no matter how they search for a workaround.

What merchants are actually trying to solve

The underlying need behind almost every reload request is a repeat-customer or loyalty scenario, not a one-off request for a single feature:

  • Store credit for returns. A merchant wants to issue a refund as credit and then keep adding to the same account balance as more returns come in, rather than issuing a new card code every time.
  • Loyalty or rewards programs. A points-to-dollars conversion that should "top up" a customer's existing balance instead of generating a new code each time they earn rewards.
  • Recurring corporate or family gifting. A parent, manager, or admin who wants to keep adding funds to the same card on a schedule rather than tracking a growing pile of separate codes.

What actually works instead

  • Issue a new gift card each time and communicate it clearly. The simplest native workaround: send a fresh code for every top-up, and make sure the customer — and your support team — know they may be holding multiple active cards.
  • Consider a dedicated store credit app if true balance accumulation is the goal. Some apps built specifically for store credit (distinct from Shopify's native gift cards) support adding to a running account balance rather than issuing new codes — worth evaluating separately if reload behavior is a core requirement, not a nice-to-have.
  • Make balance-checking effortless regardless of how many cards a customer holds. If a customer ends up with two or three codes from repeated top-ups, the practical fix isn't merging them — it's making sure they can quickly check what's left on each one without digging through old emails.

Where Gift Check Pro fits

Gift Check Pro doesn't change how Shopify issues gift cards — it can't add reload functionality Shopify's platform doesn't support. What it does solve is the practical fallout of the new-card-per-reload workaround: a simple, self-service balance check so a customer holding multiple codes from repeated top-ups can look up any of them instantly, without contacting support to ask "how much is left on this one?"

Frequently asked questions

Is there any way to add funds to an existing Shopify gift card code?

Not through Shopify's native admin or APIs. Every gift card's balance is fixed at issuance and only decreases through redemption — there's no supported action that increases it after the fact.

Do third-party apps solve this?

Some store-credit apps built on a separate account-balance model (rather than Shopify's native gift card codes) support true reloading. That's a different product category from a balance-checker for standard gift cards, so it's worth confirming which model an app actually uses before assuming it solves this.

Why doesn't Shopify just add this feature?

There's no official explanation, but the persistent, unresolved feature requests across multiple Community threads suggest it's a known, long-standing gap rather than something merchants are missing in the settings.
