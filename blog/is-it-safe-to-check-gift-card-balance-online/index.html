Is It Safe to Check Your Gift Card Balance Online? A Shopper and Merchant Guide to Avoiding Scams

Quick answer: Only check a gift card balance on the retailer's own official website, app, or in-store terminal — never on an unaffiliated third-party "balance checker" site. Merchants can reduce the risk further by giving customers a legitimate, on-site way to check a balance so they never have to go looking for one elsewhere.

Gift card fraud is a real, growing problem

Gift card scams aren't a fringe concern. Widely-cited estimates put US losses to gift card fraud and scams at around $217 million in 2023 alone, and reported scams have generally trended upward year over year. Some of that fraud starts at the point of purchase — cards tampered with on a rack — but a meaningful share also comes from people searching online for ways to check a card's balance and landing on the wrong page.

Why "checking a balance" is a scam vector

A gift card balance check requires entering a card number and PIN, which is exactly the information needed to drain a card remotely. That makes "gift card balance checker" a useful phrase for scam sites to target: a page that looks like a generic lookup tool but has no affiliation with the retailer at all, existing only to collect card numbers and PINs before a real customer gets the chance to spend the balance themselves.

For shoppers: how to tell a safe balance check from a risky one

  • Check the balance on the retailer's own domain — the store you actually bought the card from or plan to redeem it at — not a search-result link to an unrelated third-party site.
  • Be cautious of sites that ask for a card number and PIN but aren't clearly branded to a specific, recognizable retailer.
  • If a page is covered in ads, redirects you through multiple domains, or can't tell you which store the card is even for, treat it as untrustworthy.
  • When in doubt, check the balance in person at the store, or call the retailer directly instead of using a link from search results.

For merchants: the safer pattern is keeping the check on-site

Every time a customer can't easily find a balance-check option on your own storefront or at your counter, they go looking elsewhere — and "elsewhere" is exactly where scam sites are waiting. Sending customers off-site to figure out their balance, or leaving them with no self-serve option at all, doesn't just create friction. It pushes them toward exactly the kind of unaffiliated lookup pages that are a known scam vector.

A built-in, on-site or in-POS balance-check tool is the safer pattern by design. It keeps the entire lookup on a domain the customer already trusts, removes any reason to search for a third-party checker, and signals that your store takes the security of stored value seriously.

Why this matters more heading into the holidays

Gift card volume climbs sharply in the run-up to the holidays, and balance-check volume climbs right along with it. More cards in circulation means more searches for "check my gift card balance," which means more exposure to scam sites during exactly the season when gift cards are changing hands the most. Having a visible, official balance-check option in place before that volume hits is a straightforward way to keep customers from needing to look elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

What information should a legitimate balance-check page ask for?

Typically just the card number and PIN printed on the card itself, on a page clearly branded to the retailer you got the card from. It shouldn't ask for unrelated personal information, payment details, or a login to an unrelated account.

Can a scam site actually steal money from a gift card just from a balance check?

Yes. If a fake site captures a card number and PIN, whoever operates it can check the same balance and spend it before the actual owner does — the balance check itself is often the first step in that kind of fraud.

Does offering an on-site balance checker fully eliminate the risk?

No single tool eliminates fraud risk entirely, but keeping the balance check on your own domain removes the main reason a customer would go searching for a third-party site in the first place, which closes off one of the more common paths to a scam.
