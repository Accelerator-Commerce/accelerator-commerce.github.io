Every Teacher, Their Own Schedule: Running a Multi-Instructor Calendar on Shopify

Short answer: If your Shopify store is really a music school, tutoring center, or studio with several instructors each running their own lesson schedule, a single shared event list isn't enough. You need each teacher's schedule displayed on their own page, with their own dates and time zones — something most booking apps repurposed for this job don't handle cleanly.

A real question with no single good answer yet

A merchant posted a direct question in the Shopify Community: looking for the best app where each teacher conducts lessons on different days and times, needing a calendar display, per-date time zone handling, and — the key requirement — the ability for each teacher to own and manage their own calendar shown on their own introduction or bio page. The replies pointed to Sesami, BookThatApp, and Easy Appointment Booking — appointment-booking tools with staff accounts or individual calendars, but none built from the ground up as a calendar-display app organized around "one teacher, one schedule."

This keeps coming up in the education vertical specifically

It isn't a one-off. An older, related thread, Education/booking App for multi-day classes, shows the same underlying business type — a school or studio with several instructors or multi-day course sequences — hitting a related wall. Music schools, dance studios, tutoring centers, and art classes all share the same structural need: a storefront calendar that's organized by instructor first, with event scheduling as a secondary layer, not the other way around.

Why a single shared calendar doesn't work for this

A generic event calendar lists events chronologically, mixed together regardless of who's running them. That's fine for a single-instructor studio, but it breaks down the moment a business has several teachers with independent schedules: a parent looking for Tuesday guitar lessons with a specific teacher has to scroll past every other instructor's unrelated sessions to find what they're actually looking for, and there's no natural place to put a teacher's own bio, specialty, or availability pattern next to their own schedule.

What repurposed booking apps get half right

Appointment-booking apps like the ones named in the Community thread do support staff accounts, which is a real step toward per-instructor scheduling — a booking can be assigned to a specific staff member with their own working hours. Where they typically fall short for this use case is the public-facing side: staff-account scheduling is usually built around an appointment-booking flow (pick a service, pick a staff member, pick a time), not a readable, browsable, public calendar that showcases one teacher's upcoming sessions on their own page the way a touring musician's show dates or a studio's public class schedule would be displayed.

What to actually look for

  • Per-teacher calendar scoping. Each instructor's sessions should be filterable or displayed independently, not buried in one combined feed.
  • A dedicated page per teacher. A bio or introduction page with that teacher's own schedule embedded directly, so a parent or student doesn't have to cross- reference a separate master calendar.
  • Per-session time zone display. Especially for remote lessons, each session should show clearly in the viewer's local time, not just the business's home time zone.
  • Simple recurring patterns per instructor. Each teacher likely has their own weekly rhythm — the calendar should support that without forcing identical schedules across every instructor.

Where Fresh Cal fits

Fresh Cal is built as a display calendar, not a booking-and-payment engine — which is exactly the right starting point for this use case. A music school or tutoring center can set up a distinct calendar view per teacher, embed it on that teacher's own page, and keep each instructor's schedule visually separate from the others, while still living on the same storefront. For actual lesson booking and payment collection, pairing Fresh Cal's display with a booking app's scheduling backend — the same two-tool pattern covered in our booking apps vs. display calendars guide — is often the more practical setup than expecting one app to do both well.

Frequently asked questions

Can one calendar app show a different schedule for each teacher?

A display calendar like Fresh Cal can be scoped per teacher and embedded on that teacher's own page, so each instructor's schedule appears independently rather than mixed into one shared feed.

Do I need a booking app instead of a calendar app for this?

If you need students to actually book and pay for a specific slot, yes, pair a booking app for that transaction with a display calendar for the public-facing schedule. See our booking apps vs. display calendars guide for how the two fit together.

Is this the same as the yoga/fitness class-package problem?

Related but distinct. Our yoga and fitness class-package guide covers combining a class calendar with multi-class membership booking for one instructor or studio. This is specifically about multiple independent instructors each needing their own separately displayed schedule.
