Why a Yoga Studio Left Shopify for Square: The Class-Package Booking Gap

Short answer: No single Shopify app cleanly combines a scannable class calendar with multi-class package or membership booking — the thing a yoga or fitness studio actually needs. One merchant left Shopify entirely for Square and Acuity over it. You don't have to switch platforms to fix this, but you do need to pair the right two tools instead of expecting one app to do both jobs.

A merchant who actually left

In a Shopify Community thread asking for the best app to track and book yoga classes, one merchant didn't just ask for a recommendation — they reported the outcome of not finding one: "I unfortunately had to leave Shopify to go onto Square and use their Acuity App which is incredibly useful." A second reply in the same thread mentioned trying a Shopify booking app ("Tipo Appointment") for drop-in classes, but flagged a specific limitation: "it does not allow for packages unfortunately." That's a sharper, more concrete signal than a general "I wish there was a better calendar app" complaint — it's a named competitor win, with a specific missing feature identified as the reason.

Why "just book a class" isn't the whole job

A studio running recurring classes almost never sells single drop-ins as the primary product. The real business model is packages: a 5-class pass, a 10-class punch card, or a monthly membership that unlocks unlimited classes. Booking a single appointment slot is a solved problem on Shopify — plenty of apps handle one-off time-slot reservations well. What's much rarer is an app that understands a customer has already paid for a package, tracks how many classes they have left, and lets them redeem one of those classes against a specific date on the schedule.

Where existing Shopify apps fall short

The pattern in the Community thread is consistent with what shows up elsewhere in this research: booking and appointment apps are generally built around one-off, pay-per-slot reservations, not package redemption. A studio using one of these apps for classes often ends up doing one of two things — selling drop-in-priced single sessions even when they'd rather sell packages, or tracking package balances manually outside the app (a spreadsheet, a notebook at the front desk) while the booking app only manages the calendar slot itself. Neither is a real fix; both are workarounds for a gap in what a single app covers.

What to piece together instead of switching platforms

  • Use a display calendar for the public-facing schedule. Shoppers need a clean, accurate view of which classes run when — that's a calendar's job, and it doesn't need to know anything about packages to do it well.
  • Pair it with a package- or membership-aware booking or subscription app for the redemption side. Look specifically for apps that market class-pack or membership redemption, not just appointment scheduling — the Community reply above is a direct reminder to verify this before committing, since "booking app" and "package-aware booking app" are not the same claim.
  • Keep the calendar and the booking/redemption flow linked from the same page. A shopper should be able to see the schedule and get to the booking action in one place, even if two different apps are doing the work behind the scenes.

Where Fresh Cal fits — and where it doesn't

Fresh Cal is a display calendar: it's built to publish an accurate, easy-to-scan class or event schedule, including recurring weekly sessions. It isn't a booking, payment, or membership system, so it can't track class-pack balances or redeem a package against a specific date on its own. What it can do is give a studio a reliable public schedule to pair with whichever package-aware booking tool actually handles the redemption side — the visibility half of the job the Community thread's merchant needed, paired with a booking tool built for packages rather than one-off slots.

Frequently asked questions

Does Fresh Cal support class packages or memberships directly?

No — that's outside a display calendar's scope. Fresh Cal handles the schedule; pair it with a dedicated package- or membership-aware booking app for redemption and balance tracking.

Is this the same issue as choosing between a booking app and a calendar app in general?

It's a sharper version of it. The general question is whether you need reservations at all. This is about studios that already know they need bookings, but need a booking tool built around packages and memberships specifically — not just single time-slot reservations.

Do I need to leave Shopify to solve this?

The merchant in this thread did, but that's not the only path. Pairing a display calendar with a package-aware booking or membership app covers the same ground without switching ecommerce platforms — it just takes verifying the booking app actually supports packages before committing, rather than assuming any "booking app" label covers it.
