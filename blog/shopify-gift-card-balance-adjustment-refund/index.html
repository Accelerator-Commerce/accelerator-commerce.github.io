Why You Can't Just "Fix" a Shopify Gift Card's Balance (and What Happens on a Refund)

Short answer: Shopify doesn't support manually editing the balance of a gift card once it's issued — there's no admin action for it. And if a customer returns something they paid for with a gift card, Shopify can only reload the value back onto that same card automatically if the original sale went through Shopify Payments. If it didn't, the merchant has to issue a brand-new card for the refund amount instead.

Two limitations that show up at the worst possible moment

Both of these surface exactly when a merchant or customer expects a balance to just be "right": a support agent tries to correct a card that was issued with the wrong amount, or a customer returns an order and expects their gift card to simply show its money back. Neither situation has the clean fix people assume exists, and neither limitation is explained anywhere merchants are likely to find both of them together before they hit the wall.

You can't manually adjust an issued gift card's balance

A merchant asked plainly in the Shopify Community: "Is there a way to manually adjust the balance of a gift card?" The answer was direct — Shopify does not support updating the balance of a gift card once it has been issued. There's no admin field to type a new number into, no bulk correction tool, and no supported workaround that edits the original card in place. If a card was issued with the wrong amount, or a merchant wants to add a goodwill credit to an existing code, the only native path is a separate, new gift card — not a change to the one already issued.

Refund-to-gift-card only auto-reloads under one condition

The sharper, less-documented nuance shows up on returns. Shopify's own gift card guidance makes clear that automatically reloading a refund back onto the gift card a customer used to pay only works if the original transaction was processed through Shopify Payments. If the sale went through a different payment gateway, that automatic reload path isn't available — the merchant has to issue an entirely new gift card for the refund amount instead of topping up the one the customer already has. That's a gateway-level condition most merchants have no reason to know about until a return actually happens and the numbers don't behave the way they expected.

Why this catches merchants off guard

  • It looks like a bug, not a policy. A support agent seeing a gift card with the "wrong" balance reasonably assumes there's a fix screen somewhere in the admin. There isn't — the balance is fixed at issuance and only moves through redemption or a scripted adjustment via app/API-level tooling built specifically for it.
  • The refund conditional is invisible until it matters. A merchant who's always used Shopify Payments will never notice this limitation exists. One who processes even a single sale through another gateway can hit it unexpectedly on the very first return against that order.
  • Customers don't distinguish between the two. From a shopper's point of view, "my balance is wrong" and "my refund didn't show up on my card" feel like the same problem. They're actually two different platform limitations with two different explanations — and two different things a support team needs to say.

What to actually do

  • Treat "adjust the balance" requests as "issue a new card" requests. Since there's no native edit path, build that into your support script from the start rather than searching for a setting that doesn't exist.
  • Check your payment gateway before promising an automatic refund-to-gift-card. If the original order wasn't processed through Shopify Payments, tell the customer up front that the refund will arrive as a new gift card rather than a reload of their existing one.
  • Document both limitations together in your own refund policy. Because they tend to surface at the same moment (a return involving a gift card), a single clear internal note covering both saves a support agent from re-discovering each one separately mid-ticket.

Where Gift Check Pro fits

Gift Check Pro doesn't change how Shopify issues, adjusts, or reloads gift cards — those are platform-level rules an app can't override. What it does is give both customers and staff a fast, accurate way to see exactly what a card's balance is right now, whether that card is an original purchase or a new one issued to replace a refund — so "why doesn't my balance match what I expected" turns into a quick lookup instead of a support ticket.

Frequently asked questions

Can I manually change a Shopify gift card's balance in the admin?

No. Shopify has no supported way to edit the balance of a card once it's issued. The only native option is issuing a separate, new gift card.

Why didn't my customer's refund show up on their existing gift card?

Automatic reload-on-refund back to the original gift card only works when the original sale was processed through Shopify Payments. If a different gateway processed the original order, the refund arrives as a new gift card instead.

Does using a gift card affect my one-discount-code-per-order limit?

No — a gift card is a payment method, not a discount, so it doesn't count against Shopify's one-discount-code limit. The stacking confusion merchants usually run into is about combining multiple discount codes with each other, which is a separate topic with its own dedicated apps.

Are there apps that let me reload or adjust a gift card's balance?

Reloading an existing card (rather than issuing a new one) is a different, related limitation — see our guide on reloading a Shopify gift card for what actually works there. Some store-credit apps built on a separate account-balance model support true top-ups; that's a different product category from Shopify's native gift cards.
