What Actually Happens to the Money When a Shopify Gift Card Expires? A Plain-English Guide to Breakage Accounting

Short answer: When you sell a gift card, that money isn't revenue yet — it's a liability, because you owe the customer merchandise or services equal to the card's value. Revenue is only recognized when the card is actually redeemed. If a card expires (where legally allowed) or simply never gets fully used, the unredeemed portion — called "breakage" — can eventually be recognized as income, but only based on a reasonable, historically supported estimate, not just because the balance is sitting there unused.

Why a gift card sale isn't a sale, accounting-wise

It feels like a sale: a customer pays you money today. But from a bookkeeping standpoint, you haven't delivered anything yet — you've taken on an obligation to provide merchandise later, for whoever redeems the card. That's why the proceeds from a gift card sale get booked to a liability account (commonly called unredeemed gift card liability or deferred revenue), not straight to sales income. The revenue only gets recognized later, when the card is actually used to buy something — at that point the liability goes down and revenue goes up by the redeemed amount.

The mistake that quietly costs money

The most common error isn't exotic — it's a payment-method mapping problem. If a store's accounting setup routes gift card sales straight into a sales or income account instead of a liability account, the books overstate revenue the moment the card is sold, long before any merchandise actually goes out the door. For a seasonal business that sells a lot of gift cards in Q4 for redemption in Q1 — a very common pattern — this can meaningfully distort which quarter revenue actually shows up in, and overstate how much cash-on-hand is really "earned" at any given moment versus still owed to customers.

What breakage actually means

Not every dollar loaded onto a gift card gets spent. Cards get lost, forgotten, or only partially used before the remaining balance is small enough that nobody bothers redeeming it. The portion that's reasonably expected to never be redeemed is called breakage, and accounting standards allow a business to recognize a reasonable estimate of that breakage as income over time — a common starting assumption for a newer store without years of its own redemption data is somewhere in the 5–10% range, though the right number depends on your own actual redemption patterns, not an industry rule of thumb. The key word is estimate: breakage income has to be based on a defensible historical pattern, not booked speculatively just because a card hasn't been used yet.

A real question merchants are asking

This isn't just a theoretical accounting topic — merchants hit it directly. A Shopify Community thread asks plainly what happens, accounting-wise, when a gift card expires with value still left on it, without getting a clear native answer from Shopify itself. That's the gap this article is meant to close: Shopify processes the gift card transaction, but it doesn't tell you how to book the liability, the redemption, or the eventual breakage in your own accounting software — that part is on the merchant (or their bookkeeper) to set up correctly.

This is different from expiration law

It's worth being precise about what this covers versus what it doesn't. Whether a gift card is even allowed to expire, and what happens to unclaimed value, is governed by the CARD Act and state unclaimed-property (escheatment) law — that's a legal question about the cardholder's rights to the money. Breakage accounting is a separate question about how the merchant's own books reflect an unredeemed balance for financial reporting purposes. A store can be fully compliant with expiration law and still be getting its own accounting wrong, or vice versa — they're two different problems that happen to both involve the same unredeemed dollars.

It's also different from 1099-K reporting

There's a third, separate question worth distinguishing: whether a gift card sale is getting counted twice in the gross payment volume Shopify reports on your 1099-K — once at sale, again at redemption. That's a payment-processor tax-reporting issue, not an internal-bookkeeping one. See our guide on whether your gift card revenue is being taxed twice on the 1099-K for that side of it. Getting your own books right using the breakage approach above is what makes reconciling that 1099-K gap possible in the first place.

A basic walkthrough

  1. Sale: Customer buys a $100 gift card. Cash increases by $100; a $100 liability (unredeemed gift card balance) is recorded. No revenue yet.
  2. Partial redemption: Customer spends $40 of it on an order. The liability drops by $40, and $40 of revenue is recognized — the same as if they'd paid $40 in cash.
  3. Breakage recognition: Based on your store's own historical redemption data, you estimate that some fraction of remaining balances will never be redeemed, and recognize that estimated portion as income over time rather than leaving it sitting as an indefinite liability.
  4. Full redemption or legal expiration: If the remaining $60 is eventually spent, it's recognized as revenue then. If it legally expires instead (where allowed) and isn't subject to escheatment, the remaining liability is cleared out according to your breakage policy rather than left on the books forever.

Frequently asked questions

Is gift card revenue recognized when the card is sold or when it's redeemed?

When it's redeemed. The sale itself creates a liability, not revenue — revenue is only recognized as the card actually gets spent.

What percentage of gift card value typically goes unredeemed?

It varies by business and has to be based on your own historical redemption data — a commonly cited starting assumption for a store without its own history yet is in the 5–10% range, but that's a starting point for estimation, not a fixed rule.

Is breakage the same as a gift card expiring?

Related but not identical. Expiration is a legal question governed by the CARD Act and state escheat law. Breakage is an accounting estimate of how much of a card's value will realistically never be redeemed, used to recognize that portion as income over time rather than leaving it as an indefinite liability.

Does Shopify handle gift card accounting automatically?

No. Shopify processes the gift card sale and redemption, but mapping those transactions to the correct liability and revenue accounts — and estimating breakage — is a bookkeeping setup task for the merchant or their accountant, not something Shopify configures for you.
