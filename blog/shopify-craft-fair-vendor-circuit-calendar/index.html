Do the Craft Fair and Holiday Market Circuit? Put Your Pop-Up Schedule on Your Shopify Store

Quick answer: If you sell at craft fairs, farmers markets, or holiday markets, your customers want one simple answer: where will you be next? A single page on your own Shopify store listing every upcoming stop — dates, city, and location — does that job far better than a social media story that disappears in a day.

Your schedule lives everywhere except your own store

Most makers and vendors who work the circuit already announce their stops somewhere: an Instagram story, a Facebook event, maybe a pinned post. All of that works for people who happen to catch it at the right moment. None of it works for a returning customer who checks your website three weeks later wondering if you'll be at the market near them this weekend, or a new fan who found your products online and wants to see you in person before buying.

Merchants are already doing this — it's a confirmed pattern, not a guess

This isn't a theoretical use case. A real review on a Shopify event calendar app's own store listing, from a merchant with 125 reviews backing up their storefront, puts it plainly: they use the app "to show the upcoming vendor events we will be at so our customers know where to find us at our pop-up shops." That's the exact job a vendor-circuit calendar needs to do — not sell tickets, not manage RSVPs, just answer "where, and when" in a format search engines and returning customers can both find.

Why this is a different job than local SEO

It's worth separating this from the local-SEO case for listing a pop-up's dates, which is about getting found by people who don't know your brand yet, searching for something like "craft fair near me this weekend." The vendor-circuit case is about your existing audience — people who already follow you, bought from you before, or found you at a past market — who just want a reliable place to check your schedule instead of scrolling back through old social posts. Both are good reasons to publish a calendar; they're just solving different problems for different visitors.

What a vendor-circuit calendar page should include

  • Every confirmed upcoming stop, not just the next one — vendors often book three or four markets out, and customers planning ahead want to see the whole run.
  • The market or fair's name, city, and specific location, since "see you this weekend!" with no address sends people to search for it themselves.
  • A link to the market's own site or listing when one exists, so customers can check hours, parking, or entry details you don't need to duplicate.
  • A note when you'll have something new or limited at a specific stop — a reason to show up to that date specifically, not just any date.
  • Past stops removed or clearly marked as past, so the page always reads as "here's where to find me next," not a stale list.

Watch for a naming collision

If you go looking for apps to do this, be careful not to confuse a vendor-schedule calendar app with a seasonal "holiday popup" app — several tools using "holiday" or "popup" in their name are actually discount-banner or countdown-popup tools aimed at on-site promotions, not calendar apps for listing physical market appearances. They solve a completely different problem.

If your circuit runs through BFCM weekend

Markets and craft fairs cluster heavily around the holiday season, which often overlaps your Black Friday and Cyber Monday plans. If you're also running online promotions that weekend, see why a BFCM schedule needs a calendar, not just a countdown timer — the same display-calendar approach that lists your market stops can also carry your in-store BFCM hours.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a separate app if I already post my schedule on Instagram?

Social posts are still worth doing for people who already follow you, but they're not searchable or easy to scan months out. A calendar page on your own store gives you one durable, linkable place to point people — including in your Instagram bio or email signature.

How far ahead should I list my markets?

As far ahead as you've actually confirmed. Listing everything you're booked for, even months out, lets customers plan around you rather than only finding out about a market the week it happens.

What if I only do a handful of markets a year?

Even a short list is worth publishing. The value isn't in the volume of dates — it's in having one reliable place, instead of none, where someone can check before assuming you won't be anywhere nearby.
