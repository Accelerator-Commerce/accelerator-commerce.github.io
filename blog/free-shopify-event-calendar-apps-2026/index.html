Free Shopify Event Calendar Apps in 2026: What You Actually Get for $0

Quick answer: Free plans across this category are more similar than app store listings make them sound — most cap out at around five published events before you need to upgrade. The real differences on a free plan aren't event counts, they're whether you keep SEO-relevant markup, a clean mobile layout and no forced branding while you're on it.

A live, unanswered question worth answering directly

A Shopify Community thread asks, plainly: can anyone recommend a free events calendar app for listing? It's exactly the kind of question a small store or a solo artist asks before they've committed to paying for anything, and it's gone without a clear answer. Most existing "best event calendar app" roundups skip past free tiers entirely or bury the limits in fine print, which means the question keeps coming back.

The honest pattern across free plans

Free plans in this category converge on a similar shape, not a wildly different one. A five-event ceiling shows up repeatedly among calendar and booking-adjacent apps once you look past the marketing page and into the actual plan details — including Fresh Cal's own free plan, which covers your first five published events before a paid plan is needed. If you're comparing purely on "how many events do I get for free," most options in this space land in roughly the same place.

What actually varies on a free plan

Since raw event count doesn't tell you much, the differences that matter on a free tier are elsewhere:

  • Branding. Some free plans stamp the app's own name or a "powered by" badge on your calendar; others don't gate that behind a paid tier at all.
  • Structured data / SEO markup. A calendar that only adds event schema markup on paid plans is quietly weaker for search visibility even while it's free — see our guide to event structured data on Shopify for why that matters.
  • Import tools. CSV or ICS import — useful if you're migrating an existing event list from a spreadsheet, Google Calendar or another app — is a common paid-tier-only feature, so check whether you'll need it before you've hit five events.
  • Layout and styling control. Free plans sometimes lock you to one fixed layout, while paid plans unlock calendar styling to match your theme.
  • What happens past the cap. Some apps stop accepting new events once you hit the free limit; others let existing events keep showing but block adding more. That difference matters if you're a touring act adding dates gradually versus a shop publishing a fixed seasonal list.

How to actually decide on a free plan

Rather than comparing headline "free" claims, check three things directly on each app's own pricing page: whether structured data / SEO markup is included at the free tier (not just mentioned as a general feature), whether the app's branding appears on your storefront while you're on the free plan, and exactly what happens to your calendar once you cross the event count — before you build out a full schedule around it.

Where Fresh Cal fits

Fresh Cal's free plan covers your first five events, in line with the rest of the category — it isn't the outlier on event count. What it doesn't do is hold back SEO-ready event schema markup or a clean, mobile-friendly layout as a paid-only feature; those are part of the app from the free plan on. If you're a small store, a touring act just getting started, or anyone testing whether a display calendar is the right fit before committing to a paid plan, that's the practical reason to start there rather than the number of events alone.

Frequently asked questions

Is Fresh Cal's free plan bigger than competitors' free plans?

Not on event count — a five-event free tier is common across this category, Fresh Cal included. The differences worth checking are what's included alongside that limit, like schema markup and branding.

What happens when I hit the free event limit?

Check each app's own pricing page for this detail specifically — apps handle it differently, and it's exactly the kind of fine print that doesn't show up in a marketing headline.

Should I start on a free plan if I only have a handful of events right now?

Yes — a free plan is a reasonable way to confirm a display calendar fits your workflow before paying for anything, as long as you understand what's gated so you're not surprised later.
