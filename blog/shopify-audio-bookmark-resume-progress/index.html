Can Shoppers Pick Up Where They Left Off? Bookmark and Resume for Shopify Blog Audio

Short answer: Most Shopify audio players don't remember where a listener stopped — closing the tab or leaving the page loses their place, and they have to scrub back to find it manually next time. That's a real gap for anything longer than a couple of minutes, like a long-form blog post or an audiobook chapter, because Spotify, podcast apps and dedicated audiobook readers have already trained listeners to expect their progress to just be there when they come back.

Discovery already works. Continuity usually doesn't

A shopper who finds a read-aloud button on a blog post, or starts an audiobook sample on a product page, has already done the hard part — they found the audio and pressed play. What happens next, if they get interrupted, is a separate problem most Shopify audio content doesn't address at all. Close the tab, switch to another page, or just come back tomorrow, and a typical embedded player starts over from zero. For a short clip that's a non-issue. For a fifteen-minute blog post or a multi-chapter audiobook sample, restarting from the beginning is exactly the kind of friction that makes someone give up rather than finish.

Why listeners expect this now

This isn't a hypothetical preference — it's an expectation shaped by the apps people already use every day. Spotify's own community has explicitly discussed and requested reliable cross-device bookmark position for audiobooks and podcasts, treating resume-where-you-left-off as baseline functionality rather than a bonus (Spotify Community — bookmark position in an audiobook or podcast). Dedicated audiobook apps market bookmark and resume as a core, expected feature rather than something worth calling out as special. When a shopper's everyday listening habits are built around never losing their place, a storefront player that forgets everything the moment they navigate away feels broken by comparison, even though it's just missing a feature most players simply never built.

A real request, not just an assumption

There's also a direct signal from Shopify's own Community: a merchant asked for an audio player for blog articles explicitly framed around Medium's read-aloud feature (Audio player for blog articles — Shopify Community). Medium's own read-aloud experience is built around exactly this kind of continuity — a listener can leave an article and the app keeps track. A merchant reaching for that comparison is reaching for the whole experience, not just a play button.

Where this matters most

  • Long-form blog posts. A detailed guide or deep-dive article read aloud can easily run past ten minutes — long enough that most listeners won't finish in one sitting.
  • Multi-part blog series. If a shopper finishes part one and comes back for part two later, there's no reason the experience should feel disconnected from part one just because the player has no memory between visits.
  • Audiobook or long-form product-audio samples. A sample chapter long enough to want a bookmark is long enough that losing your place is a real cost, not just a small annoyance.

What resume actually requires

Getting this right isn't just adding a "resume" button — the position has to be stored somewhere that survives a closed tab, and ideally somewhere tied to the listener rather than just the browser, so it holds up across a phone and a laptop the same way Spotify's own cross-device resume does. A player that only remembers position within a single browser session solves half the problem; a listener who switches devices mid-article is right back to square one.

Where Listen Now fits

If you're publishing anything long enough that a shopper might not finish it in one sitting — a detailed guide, a multi-part series, a longer audiobook sample — treat resume as part of the listening experience, not an extra. Listen Now's player keeps track of playback position so a shopper who leaves mid-article or mid-sample can pick back up close to where they stopped instead of restarting or manually scrubbing to find their place, the same continuity they already expect from the audio apps they use every day.

Frequently asked questions

Does every audio player need bookmark and resume?

Not necessarily — a thirty-second product-audio clip doesn't need it. Anything long enough that a listener might realistically get interrupted before finishing, like a full blog post or an audiobook chapter, is where the gap actually shows up.

Is this different from just letting someone scrub back to where they were?

Yes. Manual scrubbing requires the listener to remember roughly where they stopped and hunt for it themselves. Resume means the player already knows, and picks up close to that point automatically the next time they press play.

Does resume need to work across devices, or is per-browser enough?

Per-browser resume is better than nothing, but it breaks the moment someone switches from their phone to their laptop, which is exactly the kind of gap that makes a storefront player feel behind the mainstream audio apps shoppers already use.
