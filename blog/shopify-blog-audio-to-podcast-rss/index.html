Turn Your Shopify Blog's Audio Into a Podcast (RSS Feeds for Spotify and Apple Podcasts)

Quick answer: An audio player on your blog only reaches people already on your site. To get that same narrated content into Spotify or Apple Podcasts, you need a podcast RSS feed — a standard XML file listing each episode's audio file, title, and description — which you generate once and submit to each podcast directory. Your existing blog-post audio can become that feed's episodes without re-recording anything.

You already did the hard part

If your Shopify blog has text-to-speech audio on its posts, you've already solved the part most stores never get to: turning written content into listenable audio. The step most stores miss is that this audio doesn't have to stay locked to your blog page — the same files can be packaged into a podcast that shows up in Spotify and Apple Podcasts, reaching people in an app they already use daily instead of only shoppers who happen to land on your site.

What an RSS feed actually is

A podcast doesn't live "in" Spotify or Apple Podcasts the way a blog post lives on your site — every podcast is really just an RSS feed, a plain XML file that lists each episode along with its audio file URL, title, description, and publish date. Podcast apps periodically check that feed URL and pull in any new episodes automatically. This is the same underlying mechanism blogs have used for content syndication for two decades, just applied to audio files instead of article text.

How your existing blog audio becomes podcast episodes

  • Each blog post's audio file becomes an episode. If you already have TTS-narrated audio for a post, that same audio file (or a copy of it) is what the RSS feed points to as the episode — nothing needs to be re-recorded specifically for the podcast feed.
  • The blog post's title and excerpt become the episode's title and description. Podcast directories display this text the same way search results display a page title and meta description, so it's worth treating it with the same care.
  • New posts become new episodes automatically. Once the feed exists, publishing a new blog post with audio should add a new episode to the feed without a separate manual step, the same way a new blog post automatically shows up on an RSS reader.

Submitting the feed to Spotify and Apple Podcasts

  • Spotify for Podcasters and Apple Podcasts Connect both work the same basic way: you submit your feed's URL once, the platform validates it against the podcast RSS specification, and future episodes appear automatically as the feed updates.
  • Feed validation catches most first-time issues. Missing required fields (like a podcast-level category or an episode's audio file duration) are the most common reason a first submission gets rejected — checking the feed against a podcast RSS validator before submitting saves a round trip.
  • One feed, most major platforms. A correctly formatted feed generally works across Spotify, Apple Podcasts, and most other podcast apps without needing a separate feed per platform, since they all consume the same RSS format.

Where Listen Now fits

Listen Now generates the text-to-speech audio for your Shopify blog posts, which is exactly the raw material a podcast RSS feed needs as its episodes. If you're already running Listen Now on your blog and considering the podcast-distribution step, the audio itself doesn't need to change — the additional work is packaging it into a feed and submitting that feed to Spotify and Apple Podcasts, rather than producing separate podcast-specific recordings.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a separate podcast hosting service, or can the audio stay on my Shopify store?

The audio files referenced in an RSS feed need to be reachable at a stable public URL, which your existing Shopify-hosted blog audio already satisfies in most cases — a separate podcast host isn't strictly required, though some stores use one for bandwidth or analytics reasons.

Will every blog post need to become a podcast episode?

No — you can choose to feed only certain posts (or a specific category) into the podcast RSS feed rather than every piece of blog content, the same way not every blog post needs to have TTS audio enabled in the first place.

How long does it take for a new episode to show up after publishing a blog post?

It depends on how often the podcast platform re-checks your feed — this typically ranges from within minutes to a few hours, rather than being instantaneous the moment a post is published.
