Background Music, Podcast Player, or Text-to-Speech: Which Shopify Audio App Do You Actually Need?

Short answer: These solve three different problems — ambience, content marketing and accessibility — and most "add audio to Shopify" guides treat them as interchangeable when they aren't. Picking the right one starts with naming the actual goal, not the audio format.

Why this gets confusing

Search for "how to add audio to my Shopify store" and almost every guide walks through embedding a music player, without asking what the audio is actually for. A merchant who wants a podcast player for content marketing, one who wants ambient background music, and one who wants text-to-speech for accessibility all land on the same generic tutorials — even though the right tool and the right implementation are different for each.

Background music: setting a mood

Background music is about atmosphere — a boutique or lifestyle brand wanting the storefront to feel like a physical retail space. The right implementation here is visitor-controlled: a small, visible player that a shopper can start (never autoplay), matching the accessibility guidance covered elsewhere on this site. If the goal is purely mood-setting, that's the whole requirement — no transcript, no playlist management beyond a track or two.

Podcast or audio player: content marketing

A dedicated podcast or audio player is for stores publishing recurring audio content — brand interviews, behind-the-scenes episodes, audio versions of blog posts used as a distribution channel. This use case needs more than background music does: episode organization, a persistent player that survives page navigation, and ideally a way to link out to major podcast platforms. Treating this the same as a background-music embed under-serves it — visitors expect to browse and choose an episode, not just hit one play button.

Text-to-speech: accessibility and reach

Text-to-speech turns existing written content — blog posts, product descriptions — into audio automatically, primarily to serve visitors who prefer or need to listen rather than read: people with visual or reading disabilities, multitaskers, and mobile visitors skimming while doing something else. Unlike the other two, TTS isn't really a creative or branding choice — it's an access feature, and the bar for it is different: accurate narration of the actual page content, not just "some audio on the page."

A quick way to decide

  • Want the store to feel a certain way when someone browses? That's background music.
  • Publishing recurring audio content people should be able to browse and choose from? That's a podcast/audio player.
  • Want your existing written content to reach people who prefer listening, or need to for accessibility reasons? That's text-to-speech.
  • Want more than one of these? They aren't mutually exclusive — a store can use ambient audio on collection pages and text-to-speech on blog posts at the same time.
