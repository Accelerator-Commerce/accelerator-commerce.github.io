Calendar or Ticketing App? What Your Shopify Event Business Actually Needs

Quick answer: If you're selling paid admission and need to scan people in at the door, you need a ticketing app. If you're just telling people where and when an event happens — with no ticket to sell or scan — you need a display calendar like Fresh Cal, not a ticketing platform.

A third category, not the same as booking apps

If you've read our piece on calendars vs. booking apps, you already know booking apps are for reserving a specific time slot — think one-on-one appointments or capacity-limited classes. Ticketing apps are a different category again: they exist to sell admission to an event and verify that a ticket holder actually showed up, usually with a scannable QR code. A growing cluster of Shopify apps — built natively on Shopify rather than as an embedded third-party widget — now market themselves directly as Eventbrite alternatives for exactly this job: selling tickets, issuing QR passes, and scanning them at the door with POS hardware you already own.

What a ticketing app is actually for

A ticketing app handles the full lifecycle of a paid admission event: pricing tiers, checkout, issuing a unique ticket per purchase, and a scanning step at entry that marks a ticket as used so it can't be reused. That's the right tool for a concert, a ticketed workshop, a conference, or any event where "did this person pay to get in" is a question you need answered at the door, not just a headcount you're hoping for.

What a display calendar is actually for

A display calendar like Fresh Cal answers a much simpler question: where and when is this happening? There's no ticket, no scan, no per-person record — just a clean, publishable schedule. That's the right fit for tour dates, pop-up shop hours, in-store demos, open studio hours, or any event where attendance is free and unlimited and the only job is making sure people know it's happening.

Questions to ask before you pick a category

  • Are you charging admission, or is the event free to attend?
  • Do you need to verify who actually showed up, or just publish when and where?
  • Do you need a scan-at-the-door step, with hardware or a phone camera at entry?
  • Is your main goal search-visible dates and a clean public schedule, with no transaction attached?

If you answered yes to charging admission and scanning people in, look at a dedicated ticketing app. If your honest answer is the last one, a ticketing platform is far more machinery — and checkout friction — than a free event needs.

Where the two can work together

Some merchants genuinely need both: a public calendar of upcoming appearances, some of which are free "come find us" stops and some of which are ticketed. In that case, a display calendar can still be the front door — the thing customers browse to see what's coming up — with a ticketing link attached to the specific dates that require paid admission, rather than forcing every event on the calendar through a ticketing flow it doesn't need.

Fresh Cal for Shopify

Fresh Cal is built for publishing, not selling admission — a clean, mobile-friendly calendar with SEO-ready event schema markup. For events that do need paid tickets, pair a Fresh Cal listing with a dedicated ticketing app's link rather than expecting a display calendar to take on a checkout and door-scanning job it isn't built for.

Frequently asked questions

Can Fresh Cal sell tickets or scan them at the door?

No — Fresh Cal is a display calendar for publishing event dates and details. If an event needs paid admission with a scannable ticket, that's a dedicated ticketing app's job, linked from the calendar listing rather than replacing it.

What if only some of my events charge admission?

That's common. Keep every event — free and ticketed — visible on one calendar for discoverability, and attach a ticketing link only to the dates that actually require a paid ticket.
