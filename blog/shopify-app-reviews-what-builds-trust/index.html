We Read 5,851 One-Star Shopify App Reviews: What Actually Makes Merchants Trust an App

Quick answer: A merchant's analysis of 5,851 one- and two-star Shopify app reviews across 209 apps found billing surprises were the single largest complaint category at 23%, followed by unresponsive support at 17% and apps that simply stopped working at 12%. Missing features — the thing most buyers actually compare apps on — came in last, at just 3%. Trust, not feature count, is what actually separates a good app experience from a bad one.

The data: what actually drives a one-star review

A Shopify merchant recently published a breakdown of 5,851 one- and two-star app reviews spanning 209 different apps, categorizing the actual complaint behind each low rating rather than just the star count. The results reorder what most buyers assume matters most when picking an app:

  • Billing complaints — 23%. Price changes sprung on locked-in customers, charges that continue after uninstalling, refunds that never materialize.
  • Unresponsive support — 17%. Reviewers who reported real problems and simply never got a reply.
  • App stopped working — 12%. Something that used to function broke, often after a theme or platform update, with no fix or explanation.
  • Too hard to set up — 6%. Onboarding friction severe enough to drive a one-star rating before the app was ever fully evaluated.
  • Missing features — 3%. Dead last. The thing most comparison listicles and feature-matrix pages spend the most space on barely registers as a source of real merchant frustration.

As the original analysis put it: "app quality isn't really decided by features. It's decided by billing you can trust and someone actually answering you."

Why this matters when you're comparing Shopify apps

Most app comparison content — including a lot of what ranks well in search — is built around feature checklists: does it do X, does it support Y, how does the pricing tier break down. That's not wrong, but this data suggests it's answering the wrong question. Merchants who end up genuinely unhappy with an app almost never say "it didn't have enough features." They say the billing wasn't what they expected, or nobody responded when something broke.

What this means in practice, before you install anything

  • Read the pricing terms for what happens after uninstall, not just the monthly price — continued charges after removal are a specifically named complaint pattern in the data above.
  • Check whether support responses show up in recent reviews, not just whether a "24/7 support" badge is listed on the app's page.
  • Weight feature comparisons less than you'd expect — a slightly shorter feature list from a developer with a track record of clear billing and responsive support is a safer bet than a longer list from one that doesn't.

Where this leaves Listen Now

Frequently asked questions

Where does this review data come from?

From a Shopify merchant's independent analysis of 5,851 one- and two-star reviews across 209 apps in the Shopify App Store, categorizing the actual complaint behind each low rating rather than relying on the star rating alone.

Does this mean features don't matter at all when picking an app?

Features still matter for whether an app does what you need — but this data shows they're rarely what actually causes a merchant to regret installing an app. Billing clarity and responsive support are the bigger risk factors worth checking first.

How can I check an app's billing and support track record before installing?

Sort an app's reviews by most recent and read a handful of the one- and two-star ones specifically — they tend to surface billing and support patterns quickly, even if the overall star rating looks fine.
